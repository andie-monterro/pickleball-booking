# Vietnam personal data law and the "ride home" home-address deep link

## Scope and scenario

A pickleball-court booking app is adding a "ride home" feature. A Player types their home address into the app once. The app stores that address **only** in the Player's own browser/device storage — it is never sent to or held by the venue's server or database. When the Player taps a button, their own device builds a Grab deep link (`grab://open?...&dropOffAddress=...`) using the address already sitting in the Player's browser storage, and the phone's OS hands the tap off to the Grab app directly. The venue's server is not in the request path for that hand-off; the browser/app-shell code — which the venue authored and served — reads the address out of local storage and constructs the link.

This document reports what Vietnamese primary legal sources say about that scenario, organized around five specific questions. It does not give legal advice, a recommendation, or a verdict. Where a source is silent or ambiguous, that is stated explicitly rather than filled in with inference. Every inference in this document is labeled as an inference; it is not a statement of settled law.

All citations are to the primary legal texts as retrieved from the Government Gazette (Công báo) and the Government's own legal document portal, or downloaded directly as PDFs from those portals. Full source details are in the closing section.

## 1. Is a home address personal data, and basic or sensitive?

**Short answer:** Yes, it is personal data. A self-declared, static home address is classified as **basic** personal data by Decree 356/2025/NĐ-CP, Article 3, item 4, which names "current residence" and "contact address" directly. This is a different item from the **sensitive**-data entry for "location... determined via a positioning service" (Article 4.1(h)), and the two are worded differently enough that the Decree appears to distinguish them, though it never states the distinction as a rule in so many words.

**Citations and text:**

- **Decree 356/2025/NĐ-CP, Article 3** ("Danh mục dữ liệu cá nhân cơ bản" — List of basic personal data), item 4, verbatim: *"Nơi sinh, nơi đăng ký khai sinh, nơi đăng ký thường trú, nơi đăng ký tạm trú, nơi ở hiện tại, quê quán, địa chỉ liên hệ"* ("Place of birth, place of birth registration, place of permanent-residence registration, place of temporary-residence registration, current residence, hometown, contact address"). A Player's self-typed home address is squarely "nơi ở hiện tại" (current residence) and/or "địa chỉ liên hệ" (contact address) — both appear by name in this basic-data item.
- **Decree 356/2025/NĐ-CP, Article 4.1** ("Danh mục dữ liệu cá nhân nhạy cảm" — List of sensitive personal data), item (h), verbatim: *"Vị trí của cá nhân được xác định qua dịch vụ định vị"* ("The location of an individual as determined through a positioning service"). Note: this document's brief described this item's wording as "vị trí, dữ liệu về vị trí, định vị được xác định thông qua dịch vụ định vị" — the actual decree text is shorter and reads as quoted above; the brief's phrasing was a paraphrase, not the operative text.
- **Law 91/2025/QH15, Article 31.1** gives an operative definition of "dữ liệu vị trí cá nhân" (personal location data) that lines up with Article 4.1(h): *"Dữ liệu vị trí cá nhân là dữ liệu được xác định thông qua công nghệ định vị để biết vị trí và giúp xác định con người cụ thể."* ("Personal location data is data determined through positioning technology, [used] to know a location and to help identify a specific person.") This defines the sensitive category around a *technology-derived, real-time-determined* location, not a self-declared, static text string a person types into a form.
- **Decree 356/2025/NĐ-CP, Article 3**, item 11 (the basic-list catch-all): *"Các thông tin khác gắn liền với một con người cụ thể hoặc giúp xác định một con người cụ thể không thuộc quy định tại Điều 4 Nghị định này"* ("Other information tied to a specific person or that helps identify a specific person, not falling under Article 4 of this Decree"). This catch-all is not needed here, because item 4 already names the address types directly.

**Silence/ambiguity:** The Decree lists "current residence"/"contact address" as basic data (Article 3.4) and "location... via a positioning service" as sensitive data (Article 4.1(h)/Article 31.1) using visibly different wording and different underlying mechanisms (a self-reported static fact vs. a technology-determined, presumably repeat-updatable position). Nothing in the text says outright, "a home address is never sensitive location data" — the Decree simply never uses address-type language in the sensitive list and never uses positioning-technology language in the basic list. Whether a jurisdiction's regulator would ever treat a *stored* home address as "vị trí" data if, say, an app auto-populated it from GPS rather than the user typing it, is not addressed — the scenario here is a user-typed static string, which fits Article 3.4 cleanly on the text as written.

## 2. Does client-side-only storage make the venue a controller/processor at all?

**Short answer:** The Law's definitions of "controller" and "processor" do not, on their face, hinge on the defined party receiving or holding a copy of the data — "controller" status turns on who **decides the purpose and means** of processing. That said, the Law and Decree do not address this specific fact pattern (a venue that authors client-side code but whose own server/database never receives or holds the data) at all. This is a genuine silence, and this document does not resolve it — it only reports what the definitions say and flags where they stop covering the facts.

**Citations and text — Law 91/2025/QH15, Article 2 ("Giải thích từ ngữ" — Definitions):**

- Article 2.5: *"Chủ thể dữ liệu cá nhân là người được dữ liệu cá nhân phản ánh."* ("The data subject is the person reflected by the personal data.")
- Article 2.6: *"Xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân là hoạt động tác động đến dữ liệu cá nhân, bao gồm một hoặc nhiều hoạt động như sau: thu thập, phân tích, tổng hợp, mã hóa, giải mã, chỉnh sửa, xóa, hủy, khử nhận dạng, cung cấp, công khai, chuyển giao dữ liệu cá nhân và hoạt động khác tác động đến dữ liệu cá nhân."* ("Processing of personal data is an activity that acts upon personal data, comprising one or more of the following activities: collecting, analyzing, compiling, encrypting, decrypting, editing, deleting, destroying, de-identifying, providing, publishing, transferring personal data, and other activities that act upon personal data.")
- Article 2.7: *"Bên kiểm soát dữ liệu cá nhân là cơ quan, tổ chức, cá nhân quyết định mục đích và phương tiện xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân."* ("The personal-data controller is the agency, organization, or individual that decides the purpose and means of processing personal data.")
- Article 2.8: *"Bên xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân là cơ quan, tổ chức, cá nhân thực hiện việc xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân theo yêu cầu của bên kiểm soát dữ liệu cá nhân hoặc bên kiểm soát và xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân thông qua hợp đồng."* ("The personal-data processor is the agency, organization, or individual that carries out processing of personal data at the request of the controller, or of the joint controller-processor, through a contract.")
- Article 2.9: *"Bên kiểm soát và xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân là cơ quan, tổ chức, cá nhân quyết định mục đích, phương tiện và trực tiếp xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân."* ("The joint controller-processor is the agency, organization, or individual that decides the purpose, the means, and *directly processes* personal data.")
- Article 2.10: *"Bên thứ ba là tổ chức, cá nhân ngoài chủ thể dữ liệu cá nhân, bên kiểm soát dữ liệu cá nhân, bên kiểm soát và xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân, bên xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân tham gia vào việc xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân theo quy định của pháp luật."* ("A third party is an organization or individual, other than the data subject, the controller, the joint controller-processor, or the processor, that takes part in processing personal data as provided by law.")
- **Law, Article 11.1** ("Thu thập, phân tích, tổng hợp dữ liệu cá nhân" — Collection, analysis, and compilation of personal data): *"Dữ liệu cá nhân được thu thập phải được sự đồng ý của chủ thể dữ liệu cá nhân trước khi thu thập, trừ trường hợp pháp luật có quy định khác."* ("Personal data being collected must have the consent of the data subject before it is collected, except as otherwise provided by law.") This is a dedicated article regulating "thu thập" (collection) as its own act, tied to a pre-collection consent requirement — it is not merely a sub-item folded into Article 2.6's list.
- **Law, Article 18.1** ("Các hoạt động khác trong xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân" — Other activities in processing personal data), which is where "storage" is actually named (Article 2.6's list does not include "lưu trữ"/storage): *"Bên kiểm soát dữ liệu cá nhân, bên kiểm soát và xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân, bên xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân, bên thứ ba lưu trữ dữ liệu cá nhân theo hình thức phù hợp với hoạt động của mình và có biện pháp bảo vệ dữ liệu cá nhân trong quá trình lưu trữ theo quy định của pháp luật."* ("The controller, the joint controller-processor, the processor, and the third party [each] store personal data in a form suited to their own activities, and must apply protective measures during storage as provided by law.") **Textual observation:** Article 18.1 names exactly four party types as the ones who "lưu trữ" (store) personal data under this framework — controller, joint controller-processor, processor, and third party — and does not list "chủ thể dữ liệu cá nhân" (the data subject) among them. In this scenario, the storage happens on the Player's own device, i.e., storage by the data subject. Article 18.1's enumeration does not appear to contemplate the data subject as a regulated storer of their own data at all; whether that silence means client-side storage falls outside Article 18 entirely, or whether the venue is meant to be read into one of the four listed roles anyway because it authored the storage mechanism, is not addressed by the text.

**Reasoning from the text only, flagged as inference:**

- The definition of "xử lý" (processing, Article 2.6) is activity-based and broad — it lists "thu thập" (collect) as one qualifying act among many, plus an open-ended "hoạt động khác tác động đến dữ liệu cá nhân" (other activities that act upon personal data). Nothing in Article 2.6 requires the acting party to *receive or hold* a copy of the data for the activity to count as "xử lý." **Inference:** on this wording, an act of causing data to be collected/entered into storage could, in principle, fall within "xử lý" even if the acting party's own systems never receive a copy — but the text never says this explicitly, and no example or explanatory note in the retrieved text addresses a "collection happens on code I wrote, but the data never reaches me" pattern.
- Article 2.7's controller definition ("quyết định mục đích và phương tiện xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân" — decides the purpose and means of processing) also does not require possession. A venue that decided the *purpose* (getting the Player a ride home) and authored the *means* (the code that reads local storage and builds the deep link) matches this wording on its face. **Inference:** the venue could fit the Article 2.7 controller definition by that reading, even though its server never holds the address. This is an inference from the definition's wording, not a conclusion the text states about this fact pattern.
- By contrast, Article 2.9's joint-controller-processor definition requires the same purpose/means decision **plus** "trực tiếp xử lý" (directly processes) the data. On the facts given (server never touches the data), the venue does not fit "trực tiếp xử lý" in the ordinary sense of directly handling the data itself. **Inference:** if the venue is anything under these definitions, the wording points toward "controller" (2.7) rather than "joint controller-processor" (2.9), because the direct-processing element is what the venue's architecture is specifically designed to avoid.
- Article 2.8's processor definition requires that the processor act "theo yêu cầu của bên kiểm soát... thông qua hợp đồng" (at the controller's request, through a contract). The Player's own device is not a contracted party performing processing on the venue's instructions in that sense — the Player is using the venue's code on their own device for their own purpose. **Inference:** the definitions do not appear to contemplate the Player's device as a "bên xử lý" (processor) at all; the text has no ready label for what the Player's device is doing here.

**Silence/ambiguity:** Neither the Law nor Decree 356 addresses, anywhere in the retrieved text, a fact pattern where (a) an organization authors and serves the code that causes personal data to be entered into storage, (b) that storage is exclusively the data subject's own device, and (c) the organization's own infrastructure never receives, transmits through, or holds a copy of the data. The Law's controller/processor/joint-controller-processor scheme is built around an organization that has *some* data (per Article 2.9's "trực tiếp xử lý" and Article 37's controller duties discussed in Question 3, several of which presuppose the controller has data to protect, delete, or provide). Whether a party that only supplied the code — and never the data pipeline into its own systems — counts as a "bên kiểm soát dữ liệu cá nhân" is **not answered by the text**. This document does not resolve that question; it reports that the definitions could be read either way from their bare wording, and that the specific "client-side-only, code-authored-by-venue" pattern is not discussed anywhere in the Law or the Decree.

## 3. If the venue does have obligations, what are they concretely?

**Short answer:** *If* the venue is treated as a controller (see Question 2's unresolved threshold question), Article 9 sets out detailed consent-validity conditions, Article 37 lists a controller's concrete responsibilities, and Decree 356 Article 6 sets out the required form for consent and a consent-logging duty. Several of these obligations are phrased in ways that presuppose the controller holds a record of what happened — which a client-side-only architecture, by design, does not have on the venue's own systems. The text does not address that mismatch; it is flagged here as a fact pattern the texts appear not to have anticipated, not as a violation finding.

**Citations and text — Law 91/2025/QH15, Article 9 ("Sự đồng ý của chủ thể dữ liệu cá nhân" — Consent of the data subject), in full:**

- 9.1: *"Sự đồng ý của chủ thể dữ liệu cá nhân là việc chủ thể dữ liệu cá nhân cho phép xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân của mình, trừ trường hợp pháp luật có quy định khác."* (Consent is the data subject permitting the processing of their own personal data, except as otherwise provided by law.)
- 9.2: consent is only valid when based on free will and full knowledge of: (a) the type of personal data processed and the processing purpose; (b) the controller or joint controller-processor; (c) the data subject's rights and obligations.
- 9.3: *"Sự đồng ý của chủ thể dữ liệu cá nhân được thể hiện bằng phương thức rõ ràng, cụ thể, có thể in, sao chép bằng văn bản, bao gồm cả dưới dạng điện tử hoặc định dạng kiểm chứng được."* (Consent must be expressed by a clear, specific method, capable of being printed or copied in writing, including in electronic form or a verifiable format.)
- 9.4: consent must ensure: (a) consent is given per purpose; (b) it cannot be bundled with a mandatory condition to also consent to unrelated purposes; (c) it stays valid until the subject changes it or the law provides otherwise; (d) *"Sự im lặng hoặc không phản hồi không được coi là sự đồng ý"* (silence or non-response is not to be treated as consent).
- 9.5: *"Chính phủ quy định chi tiết khoản 3 Điều này."* (The Government shall detail clause 3 of this Article.)

**Decree 356/2025/NĐ-CP, Article 6 ("Phương thức thể hiện sự đồng ý" — Method of expressing consent), the Article 9.5 delegation, in full:**

- 6.1 lists acceptable consent methods, all of which must be able to verify who consented, when, and to what: (a) in writing; (b) by a recorded call; (c) by an SMS consent syntax; (d) by email, or on a website/platform/app with a built-in technical consent-request mechanism; (đ) by other suitable methods that can be printed or copied in writing, including electronic or verifiable formats.
- 6.2: *"Bên kiểm soát dữ liệu, bên kiểm soát và xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân phải lưu trữ sự đồng ý của chủ thể dữ liệu. Trong trường hợp có tranh chấp, trách nhiệm chứng minh sự đồng ý của chủ thể dữ liệu thuộc về bên kiểm soát dữ liệu cá nhân, bên kiểm soát và xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân."* (The controller, or the joint controller-processor, must **store the data subject's consent**. In the event of a dispute, the burden of proving the data subject's consent lies with the controller / joint controller-processor.)
- 6.3: the controller/joint controller-processor may not set a default-consent mechanism, or create unclear or misleading distinctions between consenting and not consenting; any pre-existing default settings must still respect data-subject rights.
- 6.4: when seeking consent to process sensitive personal data, the data subject must be told that the data being processed is sensitive.

**Law, Article 37 ("Trách nhiệm của bên kiểm soát dữ liệu cá nhân, bên xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân, bên kiểm soát và xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân"), clause 1 (controller responsibilities), in full:** a controller must (a) spell out each party's responsibilities/rights/obligations in any agreement touching processing; (b) decide the purpose and means of processing in documents/agreements with the data subject, consistent with the Law; (c) apply appropriate management/technical measures and update them as needed; (d) report violations per Article 23; (đ) choose an appropriate processor; (e) guarantee the Article 4 data-subject rights; (g) be liable to the data subject for damage caused by the processing; (h) prevent unauthorized collection of personal data from its own systems/equipment/services; (i) cooperate with the Ministry of Public Security and competent state authorities; (k) other responsibilities under the Law and related law.

**Reusable from the video document (unchanged):** the purpose-notice principle (Article 3.2), retention tied to purpose (Article 3.3), the Article 4.1/4.5 rights and Decree 356 Article 5 response deadlines, and Article 14's deletion/refusal grounds all apply the same way here as they did for the video scenario — none of them is scenario-specific to video.

**Reasoning and silence, flagged as inference:**

- **Inference:** Article 11.1 requires consent *before* collection happens ("trước khi thu thập"). If the venue is the collector by virtue of having authored the code that causes the address to be written into the Player's storage (see Question 2), Article 11.1 would require consent be obtained before that write happens — the Player typing the address into a form the venue designed and tapping "save" plausibly satisfies this on its face, but the text does not discuss whether a user typing their own data into their own local storage, using an interface someone else coded, is the kind of "thu thập" Article 11.1 is aimed at.
- **Concrete tension flagged, not resolved:** Decree 356 Article 6.2 requires the controller to **store** the data subject's consent and to be able to prove it on dispute. Decree 330/2026/NĐ-CP Article 43.1(g) (see Question 5) fines exactly this failure — *"Không ghi nhận, không lưu trữ nhật ký về sự đồng ý của chủ thể dữ liệu cá nhân, không chứng minh được đã có sự đồng ý khi có yêu cầu..."* (failing to record or store a consent log, unable to prove consent when requested). A client-side-only architecture, by design, keeps no consent record on the venue's own systems — if a consent record exists at all, it too would live only in the Player's own device storage. **This document does not conclude that this is or is not a violation.** It reports that *if* the venue is a controller, Article 6.2's consent-storage duty and Article 43.1(g)'s matching penalty are written in a way that assumes the controller keeps its own record, and that the Law/Decree do not address a controller whose whole point is to hold no record because it holds no data.
- **Silence:** Neither the Law nor Decree 356 states whether "authoring code that reads/writes browser storage on the venue's behalf" is itself an act that triggers Article 9's or Article 3's obligations. This exact technical arrangement — code authored by one party, executed and data-held entirely on another party's (the data subject's) device — is not discussed anywhere in the retrieved text of either instrument.

## 4. Does handing the address to Grab via a deep link the Player taps carry a separate requirement?

**Short answer:** The two provisions most directly on point — Law Article 15.2(a) (provision to the data subject themself) and Decree 356 Article 7.6 (the "based on the data subject's own specific request" carve-out from "transfer") — both point toward this hand-off *not* being classified as a regulated "chuyển giao" (transfer), if the venue is treated as the provider at all. But the more basic question — whether the venue is a "providing" party in this transaction in the first place, given that its own server executes none of the hand-off — is not addressed anywhere in the text. This fact pattern (a data subject's own device, running code the venue wrote in advance, handing the data subject's own data to a third party at the data subject's own initiation, with no venue server in the path) is not discussed by the Law or Decree 356.

**Citations and text:**

- **Law, Article 15** ("Cung cấp dữ liệu cá nhân" — Provision of personal data):
  - 15.1: *"Chủ thể dữ liệu cá nhân cung cấp dữ liệu cá nhân cho cơ quan, tổ chức, cá nhân theo quy định của pháp luật hoặc theo thỏa thuận với cơ quan, tổ chức, cá nhân đó."* (The data subject provides their personal data to an agency/organization/individual as provided by law, or under an agreement with that agency/organization/individual.) **Note:** this clause frames the data subject as the one doing the "cung cấp" (providing) when they hand their own data to someone else — which is arguably the closer fit for a Player handing their own address to Grab via their own tap, rather than clause 15.2 below (which frames the *controller* as the one providing). Whether typing an address into a venue-authored form that only writes to local storage counts as "cung cấp... theo thỏa thuận" with the venue is not addressed by the text.
  - 15.2: *"Bên kiểm soát dữ liệu cá nhân, bên kiểm soát và xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân cung cấp dữ liệu cá nhân trong các trường hợp sau đây:"* (The controller, or joint controller-processor, provides personal data in the following cases:)
    - (a): *"Cung cấp cho chủ thể dữ liệu cá nhân theo yêu cầu của chủ thể dữ liệu cá nhân phù hợp quy định của pháp luật, thỏa thuận với chủ thể dữ liệu, trừ trường hợp việc cung cấp đó có thể gây tổn hại đến quốc phòng, an ninh quốc gia, trật tự, an toàn xã hội hoặc xâm phạm đến tính mạng, sức khỏe, tài sản của người khác;"* (Providing to the data subject themself, at the data subject's request, consistent with law and any agreement with the data subject — except where the provision could harm national defense, national security, public order/safety, or infringe another person's life, health, or property.)
    - (b): *"Cung cấp cho cơ quan, tổ chức, cá nhân khác khi được chủ thể dữ liệu cá nhân đồng ý, trừ trường hợp pháp luật có quy định khác."* (Providing to another agency/organization/individual when the data subject consents, unless the law provides otherwise.)
- **Decree 356/2025/NĐ-CP, Article 7.6**, verbatim: *"Cơ quan, tổ chức, cá nhân thực hiện cung cấp dữ liệu cá nhân theo khoản 2 Điều 15 của Luật Bảo vệ dữ liệu cá nhân dựa trên từng yêu cầu cụ thể của chủ thể dữ liệu thì không được coi là chuyển giao dữ liệu và không phải thực hiện theo quy định tại Điều này."* (An agency/organization/individual that carries out provision of personal data under clause 2 of Article 15 of the Law, based on the data subject's own specific request, is **not** treated as a "transfer" of data, and need not follow this Article's [transfer-agreement] requirements.) This carve-out, by its own text, covers provision under **all** of Article 15.2 — both 15.2(a) (to the data subject) and 15.2(b) (to another party, with the subject's consent) — as long as it is triggered by the data subject's own specific request.
- **Law, Article 17 ("Chuyển giao dữ liệu cá nhân")**, reused from the video document unchanged: transfer is a distinct regulated act, permitted only in the enumerated cases (consent; intra-organization sharing for the same established purpose; organizational restructuring; transfer to a processor/third party under agreement; transfer at a competent authority's request; the Article 19.1 no-consent cases), and Decree 356 Article 7.1/7.3 impose written-agreement and transparent-consent-mechanism requirements on certain of those cases.

**Reasoning from the text, flagged as inference:**

- **If** the venue is treated as the "cung cấp" (providing) party at all: the Player's tap is plausibly "yêu cầu của chủ thể dữ liệu cá nhân" (the data subject's own request) and the destination is Grab, "another organization/individual" relative to the Player, which is the Article 15.2(b) fact pattern (consent-based provision to a third party) rather than 15.2(a) (provision back to the subject). Decree 356 Article 7.6's carve-out from "chuyển giao" (transfer) would then plausibly apply, since it explicitly covers provision "theo khoản 2 Điều 15" done "dựa trên từng yêu cầu cụ thể của chủ thể dữ liệu" — and a one-tap, self-initiated ride request looks like exactly that kind of specific request. **This is an inference reasoning from the definitions to the facts; the text never discusses a ride-hailing deep link or any comparable scenario.**
- **The more basic, unresolved question:** Article 15.2 is phrased as something the **controller** does ("Bên kiểm soát dữ liệu cá nhân... cung cấp dữ liệu cá nhân"). Here, at the moment of the tap, the code executing the hand-off runs entirely on the Player's own device, reading data that has never touched the venue's infrastructure; the venue's only involvement was authoring and serving that code in advance. Whether that makes the venue the "party providing" the data under Article 15.2(b), or whether instead this is simply the Player themselves handing over their own data to Grab under Article 15.1 (with the venue not a party to the specific act of provision at all), is **not addressed anywhere in the Law or Decree 356.** Neither instrument discusses a "provider" role split between the party that authors a mechanism and the party (here, the data subject) whose device actually executes it.
- **Silence, stated plainly:** this exact fact pattern — a data subject's own device, running code a third party wrote, handing the data subject's own data to a fourth party (Grab), at the data subject's own initiation, with no server of the code-author in the path — is not addressed by Article 15, Article 17, or anywhere else in the retrieved text. This document does not conclude whether this fits "provision" (Article 15), "transfer" (Article 17), or neither because the venue is not a party to that specific act at all; the text simply does not reach this fact pattern.

## 5. Small-venue thresholds and penalty ranges

**Short answer:** The 100,000-data-subject threshold from the video document carries over unchanged. On penalties: the Law itself (Article 8) already sets fine caps directly in the statute — a mix of fixed and revenue-based amounts, which is a genuine departure from a flat-fine model. A **new, dedicated** decree — **Decree No. 330/2026/NĐ-CP**, dated 19 August 2026, "Quy định xử phạt vi phạm hành chính trong lĩnh vực an ninh mạng và bảo vệ dữ liệu cá nhân" (effective the same day) — has since been issued specifically for cybersecurity and personal-data-protection administrative sanctions, with a dedicated section (Articles 39–71) giving concrete fine amounts per violation type. As of today (14 September 2026), this decree is in force. This document verified its text directly against a PDF served from the Government Gazette portal.

**Citations and text:**

- **Reused unchanged — Law, Article 38.2–38.3 / Decree 356, Article 41**: small enterprises/start-ups may opt out of Articles 21, 22, and 33.2 for 5 years from the Law's effective date; household businesses/micro-enterprises are exempt from the same articles outright — in both cases **except** those in the personal-data-processing-services business, that **directly process sensitive personal data**, or that process data of a large number of data subjects, quantified by Decree 356 Article 41 as **100,000 or more** accumulated data subjects. This threshold is not scenario-specific and carries over unchanged.
  - **New, text-based link to Question 1's finding**: since a self-declared home address is *basic* personal data (Decree 356 Article 3.4) rather than *sensitive* (Article 4.1(h)), collecting home addresses for the ride-home feature does not, by itself, trigger the "directly processes sensitive personal data" ground that would strip a small venue of the Article 38.2/38.3 carve-out. This is a one-step inference connecting two already-cited provisions, not a new textual finding.

- **Law 91/2025/QH15, Article 8** ("Xử lý vi phạm pháp luật về bảo vệ dữ liệu cá nhân" — Handling violations of personal-data-protection law), in full:
  - 8.1: violators may face administrative penalties or criminal prosecution depending on the nature/severity/consequences of the violation, and must pay damages if harm results.
  - 8.2: administrative penalties in this field follow clauses 3–7 of this Article and the general law on handling administrative violations.
  - 8.3: *"Mức phạt tiền tối đa trong xử phạt vi phạm hành chính đối với hành vi mua, bán dữ liệu cá nhân là 10 lần khoản thu có được từ hành vi vi phạm..."* (The maximum fine for buying/selling personal data is **10 times the proceeds obtained from the violation**; if there is no such proceeds, or the proceeds-based amount is lower than clause 5's cap, clause 5's cap applies instead.)
  - 8.4: *"Mức phạt tiền tối đa... đối với tổ chức có hành vi vi phạm quy định chuyển dữ liệu cá nhân xuyên biên giới là 5% doanh thu của năm trước liền kề của tổ chức đó..."* (The maximum fine for an organization's cross-border personal-data-transfer violation is **5% of that organization's revenue in the immediately preceding year**; same fallback to clause 5's cap if there is no such revenue or the revenue-based amount is lower.)
  - 8.5: *"Mức phạt tiền tối đa... đối với các hành vi vi phạm khác trong lĩnh vực bảo vệ dữ liệu cá nhân là 03 tỷ đồng."* (The maximum fine for other violations in this field is **3 billion VND**.)
  - 8.6: the clause 3–5 caps apply to organizations; an individual committing the same violation faces a maximum fine of **half** the organizational maximum.
  - 8.7: the Government shall prescribe the method for calculating proceeds from a violation.
  - **This is a genuine departure from a flat-fine model**: alongside the 3-billion-VND fixed cap (8.5), the Law introduces revenue-based and proceeds-based fines (8.3, 8.4) for specific violation types — a GDPR-style mechanism this document has not seen in Decree 13/2023's own text (Decree 13/2023 had no penalty schedule of its own; this document did not independently re-verify Decree 15/2020/NĐ-CP's or Decree 14/2022/NĐ-CP's exact fixed-fine figures in this research session, so the "flat-fine model" comparison rests on the general background described in the task brief, not on a primary source checked here).

- **Decree No. 330/2026/NĐ-CP** (new primary source for this document, not covered by the video document): dated 19 August 2026, "Quy định xử phạt vi phạm hành chính trong lĩnh vực an ninh mạng và bảo vệ dữ liệu cá nhân" (Regulating administrative sanctions in the fields of cybersecurity and personal-data protection), 4 chapters, 82 articles, effective from its issuance date (Article 80: *"Nghị định này có hiệu lực thi hành kể từ ngày 19 tháng 8 năm 2026"*). As of the reporting date (14 September 2026) it is in force.
  - **Article 7.4** mirrors Law Article 8's caps almost verbatim inside the Decree itself (10x proceeds for buying/selling data; 5% of prior-year revenue for cross-border-transfer violations by an organization; 3 billion VND cap for other violations; individuals at half the organizational rate).
  - **Article 7.1** draws a structural distinction: for the **cybersecurity** sections (Chapter II, Sections 1–5), the stated fine figures are the **individual** rate, and an organization committing the same act pays double. For the **personal-data-protection** section (Chapter II, Section 6, Articles 39–71), it is the reverse: *"Mục 6 Chương II Nghị định này quy định về mức phạt tiền được áp dụng đối với hành vi vi phạm hành chính trong lĩnh vực bảo vệ dữ liệu cá nhân do tổ chức thực hiện. Trường hợp cá nhân có cùng hành vi vi phạm, mức phạt tiền được áp dụng với cá nhân bằng một phần hai lần mức phạt tiền áp dụng đối với tổ chức."* (Section 6's stated figures are the **organization** rate; an individual committing the same act pays half.) This means the specific VND figures quoted below already apply to the venue as an organization, as written.
  - **Article 43** ("Vi phạm quy định về sự đồng ý của chủ thể dữ liệu cá nhân" — Violations of consent requirements): 30–50 million VND for a list of consent-process failures including (a) processing after collection without valid consent; (c) default-consent dark patterns; (g) *"Không ghi nhận, không lưu trữ nhật ký về sự đồng ý của chủ thể dữ liệu cá nhân, không chứng minh được đã có sự đồng ý khi có yêu cầu..."* (failing to record/store a consent log or prove consent on request); 50–70 million VND for continuing to process after a stop/restrict request, or treating silence as consent.
  - **Article 48** ("Vi phạm quy định về thu thập dữ liệu cá nhân" — Violations of collection requirements): 30–50 million VND for collecting outside the stated scope/purpose or without prior consent; escalating to 100–800 million VND, scaled by the number of data subjects and whether the data is sensitive, for unlawful collection carried out through applied technology/technical means.
  - **Article 49** ("Vi phạm quy định về cung cấp dữ liệu cá nhân" — Violations of provision requirements): 10–20 million VND for refusing to provide a data subject their own data on valid request; 20–30 million VND for providing data to another organization/individual without the data subject's consent; double for sensitive data.
  - **Article 52** ("Vi phạm quy định về chuyển giao dữ liệu cá nhân" — Violations of transfer requirements): 20–30 million VND for an incomplete transfer agreement or unsupervised intra-organization sharing; 30–50 million VND for fee-based transfers under Article 17.1(a)/(d) that skip the Article 7.1/7.3 obligations; 50–80 million VND for transferring sensitive data without the required security measures.
  - **Article 1.3**: where one act would violate both this Decree and a sector-specific sanctions decree for a different field, *this* Decree applies whenever the violation falls in the cybersecurity or personal-data-protection field, and the other sector decree applies otherwise.

**Silence/ambiguity:**
- This document searched the full text of Decree 330/2026/NĐ-CP (82 articles, retrieved directly as a PDF) for any mention of Decree 15/2020/NĐ-CP or Decree 14/2022/NĐ-CP (the general ICT-sanctions decrees that filled the gap while Decree 13/2023 had no penalty schedule of its own) and found **zero** references to either by number. Decree 330's Article 1.3 (quoted above) resolves *overlap* in favor of Decree 330 for conduct classified within the personal-data-protection or cybersecurity fields, which functionally displaces reliance on Decree 15/2020/14/2022 for that conduct going forward — but this document did not find, and could not verify, an explicit repeal clause naming Decree 15/2020 or 14/2022. Whether those decrees' personal-data-adjacent provisions were formally repealed elsewhere, or simply superseded in practical effect by Article 1.3's overlap rule, is **not confirmed by the text retrieved here** and is flagged rather than guessed.
- There is also a roughly 7.5-month gap between the Law/Decree 356 taking effect (1 January 2026) and Decree 330 taking effect (19 August 2026). This document did not investigate what sanctions regime, if any, applied to personal-data-protection violations specifically during that gap window, since the reporting date (14 September 2026) falls after Decree 330 took effect and the gap is not relevant to the current state of the law.
- No provision in either the Law or Decree 330 is scenario-specific to a client-side-only architecture; the fine figures above (Articles 43, 48, 49, 52) are the general-purpose consent/collection/provision/transfer violation categories, and this document does not assert that any of them actually applies to the ride-home feature — that would require resolving Question 2's and Question 4's open threshold questions first, which the primary texts do not do.
